commonFaults:
  - title: Passing when shut
    text: Scored or worn seats let fluid past the closed ball, often after the valve has been used part open.
  - title: Leaking at the stem
    text: Worn stem seals or packing let fluid escape where the lever or actuator connects.
  - title: Stiff or seized
    text: Scale, deposits or swollen seats make the ball hard to turn, or lock it in one position.
  - title: Body joint leak
    text: On multi-piece valves the seal between the body sections can fail and weep.
  - title: Damaged ball
    text: Erosion or scratches on the ball stop it sealing, even against new seats.

A ball valve is usually repaired by renewing the parts that seal. The valve is taken apart, the ball, seats and stem are cleaned and examined, and anything worn or damaged is repaired or replaced. Which parts need attention depends on what we find once it is open.

If the valve has an actuator, the two can be repaired together or separately, as you prefer. The [actuator & cylinder repair](/valve-repair/actuator-cylinder-repair/) page covers the actuator side.

Before it leaves us, the valve is tested, and a report of the work is sent with it. If the body or ball is too badly damaged to be worth repairing, we will tell you and can quote for a new [ball valve](/products/manual-valves/ball-valve-flanged-end/) instead.

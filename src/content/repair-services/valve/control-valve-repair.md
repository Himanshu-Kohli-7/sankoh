commonFaults:
  - title: Passing when closed
    text: Wear on the plug and seat lets flow through when the controller asks the valve to shut.
  - title: Sticking or jerky movement
    text: Tight packing, a bent stem or deposits on the trim make the stem move in steps rather than smoothly.
  - title: Hunting around the set point
    text: The valve keeps overshooting and correcting, often because of friction, a worn actuator or a positioner out of adjustment.
  - title: Gland leak
    text: Process fluid escapes past worn packing around the stem.
  - title: Noise and vibration
    text: Worn or loose trim can rattle in the flow, and the damage grows the longer it runs.

A control valve is repaired as a complete assembly: the valve body and trim, the actuator that moves it and the positioner that sets its opening. A fault that shows up as poor control can start in any of the three, so we look at them together rather than one part on its own.

The body is opened and the plug, seat, stem and packing are examined. Parts worn by the flow are repaired or replaced, the actuator is checked for air leaks and lost force, and the positioner is set up again so the valve follows its control signal over the full stroke. The [positioner repair & calibration](/valve-repair/positioner-repair-calibration/) and [actuator & cylinder repair](/valve-repair/actuator-cylinder-repair/) pages cover those parts in more detail.

The assembly is tested before it leaves our workshop, and you get a written report of the work done on it.

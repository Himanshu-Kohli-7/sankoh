faqs:
  - question: Why do globe valves on steam lines start to pass?
    answer: High-velocity steam through a partly open valve cuts into the disc and seat, a kind of wear called wire-drawing. Once those surfaces are scored, the valve no longer shuts tight.

    answer: It depends on the damage. Many globe valves come back into service once the disc and seat are repaired or replaced, while a cracked or heavily eroded body is usually a reason to replace. We tell you which once the valve is open.

Globe valves throttle flow between a disc and a seat, so those two surfaces take most of the wear. In a repair the bonnet comes off, the disc, seat, stem and packing are cleaned and examined, and any part that no longer seals is repaired or replaced. The gland is repacked and the bonnet joint renewed where needed.

For a motorised globe valve, the actuator is checked as part of the same job. Globe-pattern valves used for modulating control are covered on the [control valve repair](/valve-repair/control-valve-repair/) page.

Every globe valve is tested before dispatch and goes back with a report of the work. If the body itself is cracked or badly eroded, we will say so and can quote for a new [globe valve](/products/manual-valves/globe-valve/).

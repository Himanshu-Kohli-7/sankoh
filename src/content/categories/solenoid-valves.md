howToChoose:
  - title: Match the principle to the line pressure
    text: Direct acting and semi-lift valves open from zero pressure. Pilot operated valves need some pressure difference across them, but pass far more flow from a small coil. If the line can fall to almost nothing, such as the outlet of a gravity tank, avoid a plain pilot operated valve.
  - title: Normally closed or normally open
    text: Decide which position leaves the process safe if the power is lost, and which state the valve sits in most of the day. A coil that stays energised for long spells runs warm, so pick the form that keeps it switched off most of the time.
  - title: Pick seals for the media and its temperature
    text: Diaphragm and seal compounds differ for water, oils, gases, chemicals and steam. Hot water and steam need heat-resistant seals and a high-temperature coil together. Tell us what flows through the valve and how hot it gets.
  - title: Confirm the coil voltage and its connection
    text: Every coil is wound for one AC or DC voltage. Check what the panel supplies, how the wiring will join the coil, and how well sealed it must be for its spot on the plant.
  - title: Keep the line clean
    text: Pilot and bleed holes are small and block easily. A strainer ahead of the valve, and clean, dry air where the valve switches compressed air, prevent most of the faults that look like a failed solenoid.
faqs:
  - question: How is a solenoid valve different from a motorised valve?
    answer: A solenoid valve switches in a fraction of a second and is either fully open or fully shut. A motorised valve takes several seconds to travel and can hold positions in between. Solenoids suit smaller lines and fast on/off switching; motorised valves suit larger bores and throttling.
  - question: Can a solenoid valve control the rate of flow?
    answer: A standard solenoid valve is on/off only. Where the flow has to be set anywhere between shut and open, choose a control valve fitted with a positioner instead.
  - question: Why does a solenoid valve hum or buzz?
    answer: A soft hum from an AC coil is normal. A loud buzz usually means the plunger is not pulling fully home, because of dirt in the tube, a damaged shading ring or low supply voltage. Deal with it early, because a coil that cannot seat its plunger overheats.
  - question: Which solenoid valve switches air to a pneumatic actuator?
    answer: A 3/2 way valve suits a spring-return actuator, and a double-acting actuator usually takes a 5/2 way valve, often mounted straight onto the actuator. Tell us the actuator model and we will match the valve to it.

A solenoid valve opens or shuts a line the moment its coil is switched on or off, with no motor, gearbox or air supply of its own. That makes it the simplest way for a panel, PLC, timer or level switch to control a fluid, and the usual way to switch the air that drives [pneumatic actuated valves](/products/pneumatic-actuated-valves/).

The range runs from small direct acting valves for low flows and pilot air, through pilot operated and semi-lift diaphragm valves for water and general service, to piston type valves for steam and pulse jet valves for cleaning dust collector filters, along with replacement coils. Typical users include water treatment plants, HVAC and building automation systems, industrial automation and water and milk ATMs. Depending on the valve, the media can be air, water, oil, gas, chemicals or steam.

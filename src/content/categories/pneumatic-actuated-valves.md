  - title: On/off or control
    text: Decide whether the valve only has to open and close, or hold positions in between. Quarter-turn ball, butterfly and plug valves are mostly used for on/off duty. A globe control valve with a positioner is built for steady regulation of flow, pressure or temperature.
  - title: Pick the fail position
    text: Think about what should happen if the air supply or the control signal is lost. A spring-return actuator drives the valve to a set position, open or closed. A double-acting actuator stays where it was, which is fine for many lines but not for safety-critical ones.
  - title: Match the valve to the media
    text: Sanitary lines in dairy, food and pharma need tri-clover clamp ends that strip down for cleaning. Corrosive water and dosing lines often suit UPVC bodies. Steam and hot water are a common job for Y-type angle seat valves. Thick or dirty media may call for a plug valve.

faqs:
  - question: Should I choose a pneumatic or an electric actuated valve?
    answer: Pneumatic actuators are fast, simple and well suited to frequent cycling, and they can fail safe with a spring. They need a compressed air supply. Electric actuators need only power and suit sites without air, but they are usually slower. If your plant already has instrument air, pneumatic is often the simpler choice.

Pneumatic actuated valves use compressed air to open, close or position a valve, so a line can be controlled from a panel, PLC or control system instead of by hand. They switch quickly, cycle reliably many times a day and can be set to move to a safe position when air or signal is lost. That makes them a standard part of water treatment, HVAC and building automation, industrial automation and water and milk ATMs, on air, water, oil, gas, chemical and steam lines.

The range covers quarter-turn ball, butterfly and plug valves for automatic isolation, globe control valves for regulating flow, pressure and temperature, Y-type angle seat valves for steam and hot water, sanitary tri-clover and corrosion-resistant UPVC valves, and power cylinders that drive dampers.

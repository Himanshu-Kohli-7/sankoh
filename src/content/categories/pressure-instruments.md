faqs:
  - question: What is the difference between gauge, absolute and differential pressure?
    answer: Gauge pressure is measured against the surrounding atmosphere, which is what most process gauges show. Absolute pressure is measured against a perfect vacuum. Differential pressure is the difference between two points in the process, such as either side of a filter.
  - question: Should I choose a gauge or a transmitter?
    answer: A gauge is enough when someone only needs to look at the pressure on site. Choose a transmitter when the reading has to reach a control system, be recorded or trigger action from a distance. A transmitter with a local display can do both.
  - question: Why does a gauge pointer shake?
    answer: Pulsation from a pump or compressor, or vibration of the pipework. A liquid-filled gauge, a snubber or a partly closed needle valve under the gauge steadies it and stops the movement wearing out.
  - question: Do pressure instruments need regular checking?
    answer: Yes. Elements and sensors drift over time, especially after overpressure or heavy pulsation. A periodic comparison against a reference keeps readings trustworthy.

Pressure instruments tell you what the pressure in a line, vessel or filter is, and what to do about it. A [pressure gauge](/products/pressure-instruments/pressure-gauge/) gives the operator a reading on the spot. A pressure switch acts on a set point, starting a pump or shutting down a compressor. Pressure and differential pressure transmitters send a continuous signal to a PLC or control room. Differential pressure is also widely used to work out flow and level.

Accessories matter as much as the instrument itself. A siphon protects a gauge from live steam, and a [needle valve](/products/manual-valves/needle-valve/) isolates it so it can be changed without shutting the line.

These instruments are fitted across boiler houses, compressed air systems, pump stations, water treatment plants, HVAC and building automation and wider industrial automation.

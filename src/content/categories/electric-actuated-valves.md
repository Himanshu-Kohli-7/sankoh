howToChoose:
  - title: Quarter-turn, multi-turn or linear
    text: The valve decides the actuator. Ball, butterfly and plug valves need a quarter turn. A gate valve needs many turns of its stem, and a globe valve needs a straight push and pull. Each takes a different type of electric drive, so start from the valve the line calls for.
  - title: Open/close or modulating
    text: For plain isolation, an on/off actuator runs to fully open or fully shut and stops on its limit switches. To hold a valve part-open, ask for a modulating actuator that follows an analogue signal from your controller and reports its position back.
  - title: Decide what happens when power fails
    text: A standard electric actuator simply stops where it is when the supply is lost. If the line must close or open by itself, you need a fail-safe option such as a spring-return or battery-backed actuator. Say so at enquiry, because it changes the actuator selected.
  - title: Give us the supply and the control signal
    text: Electric actuators are built for particular voltages, so tell us the supply available at the valve. Tell us too how the panel, PLC or building management system will command it, and what feedback it expects to receive.
  - title: Think about where it will sit
    text: An actuator outdoors, in a washdown area or near corrosive fumes needs a suitably sealed enclosure, and a hazardous area needs one certified for that zone. Mention the location, the ambient temperature and how often the valve will operate.
faqs:
  - question: When is an electric actuated valve the better choice than a pneumatic one?
    answer: When the site has electricity but no reliable compressed air, such as pump houses, remote water schemes, HVAC plant rooms and water or milk ATMs. Running a cable is usually simpler than laying air lines to a few scattered valves. Where instrument air is already on hand and the valve must move fast or cycle constantly, pneumatic is usually preferred.
  - question: How fast does an electric actuator open and close a valve?
    answer: Slower than a pneumatic one, typically several seconds or more for a quarter turn and longer for a gate valve. The steady movement is often an advantage, because it reduces surge and water hammer when a line opens or shuts.
  - question: Can a motorised valve be operated by hand?
    answer: Yes. Electric actuators normally carry a handwheel or a manual override so the valve can be moved during commissioning, maintenance or a power cut. Most also show the valve position on a dial or indicator on top.
  - question: Can you motorise a valve we already have?

Electric actuated valves, often called motorised valves, use an electric motor and gearbox to open, close or position a valve on a command from a panel, PLC or building management system. They need only a power supply and a control cable, which makes them the natural choice wherever compressed air is not available or not worth installing. A handwheel lets an operator move the valve by hand when the power is off.

The range covers quarter-turn ball, wafer butterfly and spherical disc butterfly valves, hygienic tri-clover valves for dairy and food lines, corrosion-resistant UPVC valves, and motorised plug, globe and gate valves. You will find them on water treatment plants, in HVAC and building automation, across industrial automation, and inside water and milk ATMs, handling air, water, oil, gas, chemicals and steam.

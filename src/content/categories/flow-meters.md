  - title: Start with the fluid
    text: Each measuring principle suits a different kind of fluid. Electromagnetic meters need a liquid that conducts electricity, vortex meters handle steam and gas well, turbine meters want clean, thin liquids, and mass meters read the flow by weight directly. Rule out the principles that cannot see your fluid before comparing anything else.
  - title: Know the lowest and highest flow
    text: A meter is only accurate between its minimum and maximum flow. Give us the normal, lowest and highest rates you expect, not just the pipe size, because a meter sized to the pipe is often too big for the real flow.
  - title: Check the straight pipe available
    text: Bends, valves and pumps disturb the flow profile, and most meters need some straight pipe before and after them to read properly. If space is tight, say so early, since some principles tolerate short runs better than others.
  - title: Decide what the reading must do
    text: A local display is enough for a spot check. Feeding a PLC, a batching panel or a billing record needs an output signal, and sometimes a totaliser or a communication link. Remember where the power will come from, too.
  - title: Plan for cleaning and access
    text: Slurries, scale and sticky liquids foul some meters faster than others. Think about how the meter will be removed, cleaned or checked, and whether a bypass line is needed so the process can keep running meanwhile.

A flow meter measures how much liquid, gas or steam passes along a pipe, either as a rate or as a running total. Plants use that reading to control dosing and batching, to share utility costs between departments, to track losses and to prove what was delivered.

No one principle suits every fluid. [Electromagnetic](/products/flow-meters/electromagnetic-flow-meter/) and ultrasonic meters read conductive liquids with nothing in the bore to wear out. Vortex meters measure steam, gas and liquids from the swirls shed behind an obstruction. Turbine meters count the turns of a rotor in clean fluids. Mass meters read the flow by weight, and water meters keep a simple running total on supply and distribution lines.

Flow measurement matters on water treatment plants, in the dispensing lines of water and milk ATMs, on HVAC and building automation circuits and across industrial automation.

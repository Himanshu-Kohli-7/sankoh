  - title: Start from the actuator
    text: Accessories bolt onto the actuator, so its mounting pattern, shaft and stroke decide what fits. Give us the make, model and size, or a photo of the actuator's top and nameplate.
  - title: On/off or modulating duty
    text: An on/off valve usually needs only a limit switch box to confirm its end positions. A valve that throttles needs a positioner, plus feedback of its true position if the control system has to see it.
  - title: Match the signals both ways
    text: Note the signal your controller sends, such as a current loop or a digital protocol, and what it expects to get back. A positioner, I/P converter or feedback unit has to speak the same language as the panel.
  - title: Where will it live?
    text: Rain, hose-down cleaning, chemical fumes and dust all call for a well-sealed housing, and a flammable atmosphere calls for a unit approved for that classification. Describe the site when you enquire.
  - title: Look after the air
    text: Positioners and I/P converters have fine internal passages. Clean, dry, oil-free instrument air, with a filter regulator ahead of them, does more for their life than anything else.
faqs:
  - question: Can accessories be added to an actuator that is already installed?
    answer: Usually. Most quarter-turn actuators have a top shaft and mounting holes meant for a switch box or positioner, fitted with a bracket and coupling. Linear actuators take a bracket on the yoke and a feedback lever on the stem.
  - question: What is the difference between a positioner and an I/P converter?
    answer: An I/P converter only turns a current signal into a matching air pressure. A positioner compares the signal with where the valve actually is, and keeps adjusting the air until they agree, so it overcomes friction and changing loads.
  - question: Does every automated valve need a limit switch box?
    answer: Not always, but it is the only way for the control system to confirm that the valve really moved. On interlocked sequences, batching and critical isolation duties, that confirmation is well worth having.

An actuator turns a valve, but on its own it cannot tell the control room where the valve is, hold a part-open position accurately, or act on a current signal from a controller. Valve automation accessories fill those gaps. They mount on the actuator or the valve and link it to the panel, PLC or control system.

The range covers limit switch boxes that confirm open and shut, smart valve positioners for modulating control, I/P converters that turn a current signal into air pressure, and add-on position feedback and local control units that suit electric actuators. It also includes air release valves that keep trapped air out of water pipelines. You will find them across water treatment, HVAC, building automation and industrial automation.

faqs:
  - question: Radar or ultrasonic, which should I choose?
    answer: Both are non-contact and read from above. Radar copes with vapour, steam, dust, pressure and changing temperature, which upset an ultrasonic transmitter, so it suits harder duty. Ultrasonic is often the economical choice for open water tanks, sumps and channels.
  - question: Does foam on the surface affect the reading?
    answer: Heavy foam can soak up the microwave signal and weaken the echo. Light foam is usually fine. Mention foaming when you enquire, since antenna choice and mounting make a difference.
  - question: Where should it be mounted on the tank?
    answer: Away from the inlet stream, ladders, agitator blades and the tank wall, so the beam sees the surface rather than fittings. The antenna should project fully below the nozzle.

A radar level transmitter measures the level of a liquid or bulk solid from above, without touching it. Mounted on the roof of a tank or silo, it sends microwave signals down to the surface and picks up the reflection. The time the signal takes to travel down and back gives the distance to the surface; subtracting that from the tank height gives the level.

Microwaves are hardly affected by the air, vapour, pressure or temperature above the product, which makes radar dependable in closed process vessels, hot tanks and dusty silos. How well a surface reflects depends on its dielectric constant: water and most chemicals give strong echoes, while oils and some powders give weaker ones.

The antenna focuses the beam. Horn, rod and lens types suit different nozzle sizes and conditions. The transmitter sends level, volume or percentage to a PLC or display.

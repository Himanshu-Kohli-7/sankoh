A vibrating fork level switch detects when a liquid or powder reaches a set point. Two metal tines, shaped like a tuning fork, are driven to vibrate at their natural frequency by a piezoelectric drive in the base. In air the fork vibrates freely.

When the product covers the tines, it damps them and lowers the frequency. The electronics notice the change and switch the output. When the product falls away, the frequency returns and the output switches back. This makes it a dependable high-level, low-level or dry-run switch.

There are no moving parts to wear or jam, and calibration on site is seldom needed for most liquids. Forks cope with turbulence, bubbles, light foam and many coatings, and they work in any mounting position, from the top, the side or inside a pipe.

A side mounted level transmitter fits through a nozzle or flange in the wall of a tank. A float on a hinged arm sits inside, riding on the liquid. As the level changes, the float lifts or drops and the arm swings about its pivot at the tank wall.

That movement is carried through the wall, often by a magnetic coupling, so the inside of the tank stays sealed from the enclosure outside. In a transmitter, the arm angle is converted into a continuous signal that represents the level across the band the float can travel. Similar side-mounted designs with switch contacts give alarms or start and stop pumps at a set level.

The design is simple, rugged and independent of foam, vapour or dust above the liquid. It needs a liquid dense enough to float on and enough room inside the tank for the arm to swing freely.

faqs:
  - question: Which liquids does it work with?
    answer: Conductive liquids such as tap water, raw water, effluent, brine and most acids and alkalis. It will not work on oil, diesel, solvents or very pure DM or RO water, which do not conduct enough current. For those, use a float, fork or capacitance switch.
  - question: How does one switch control a pump on and off?
    answer: With three electrodes of different lengths. The longest is the common, a medium one sets the pump-on (or low) level and a short one sets the pump-off (or high) level. The relay latches between the two, so the pump runs over the whole band instead of cycling.
  - question: Will scale or dirt on the electrodes cause problems?
    answer: Light deposits are usually tolerated, but heavy scale or grease can insulate the rods and stop the switch working. Wipe them at routine maintenance, and adjust the relay's sensitivity for liquids that conduct poorly.
  - question: Is it safe to have electricity in the water?
    answer: The controller uses a low-voltage alternating current at a very small current between the electrodes. Alternating current also prevents the rods from corroding by electrolysis.

A conductivity level switch, also called an electrode or probe type level switch, senses level by checking whether a liquid completes an electric circuit. Metal rods or suspended electrodes hang into the tank from a holder at the top, each cut to the height of a switching point. A controller applies a small alternating voltage between each electrode and the tank wall or a common rod.

While the electrode hangs in air, no current flows. When conductive liquid rises to touch its tip, a tiny current passes through the liquid, and the controller's relay operates. With electrodes at different lengths, one holder can give several levels, for example pump on, pump off and high alarm.

There are no moving parts to stick, and the electrodes are easy to shorten for a new level. The method only works on liquids that conduct, so it suits water and effluent but not oils or very pure water. It is not related to the [conductivity & TDS analyzer](/products/analytical-instruments/conductivity-tds-analyzer/), which measures water quality rather than level. For non-conductive liquids, use a [float type level switch](/products/level-instruments/float-type-level-switch/).

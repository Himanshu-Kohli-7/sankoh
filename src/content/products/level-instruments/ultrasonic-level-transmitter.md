faqs:
  - question: What is the blocking distance?
    answer: Right after it sends a pulse, the sensor is still ringing and cannot hear an echo. Any surface closer than this short distance cannot be read, so the sensor must be mounted at least that far above the highest level.
  - question: Will it work in a closed tank with steam or vapour?
    answer: Not well. Vapour, steam and pressure change the speed of sound and absorb the pulse. Radar is the better option in those conditions.
  - question: Can it measure flow in an open channel?
    answer: Yes. Mounted above a flume or weir, it reads the water height, and the transmitter or controller converts that height to flow using the shape of the structure.

An ultrasonic level transmitter measures level from above by sound. A sensor on top of the tank sends a short pulse of ultrasound towards the surface. The pulse bounces back, and the electronics time the echo. Knowing the speed of sound, they work out the distance to the surface and from it the level.

Because nothing touches the liquid, the sensor stays clean on sewage, effluent and corrosive chemicals. It is a simple, economical way to watch water tanks, sumps and open channels.

The speed of sound changes with air temperature, so a built-in temperature sensor corrects for it. Heavy foam, thick vapour, dust and strong wind across an open sump can weaken or scatter the echo, and the beam needs a clear path free of ladders, pipes and the inlet stream.

A rotary paddle level switch tells you when a silo, bin or hopper of bulk solids is full or nearly empty. A small, low-speed motor in the enclosure turns a paddle on a shaft inside the vessel. In air it turns freely.

When the material rises and buries the paddle, it resists the turning. The motor is mounted so that this resistance makes it rotate slightly against a spring, which operates a microswitch. That contact signals the level and usually switches the motor off until the material falls away again. Because it works by resistance to rotation, it is also known as a resistance rotary level switch.

It suits cement, ash, grain, flour, granules and other dry solids. Paddle size and shape are chosen to suit the bulk density of the material, and a slip clutch or flexible coupling usually protects the drive from shock loads.

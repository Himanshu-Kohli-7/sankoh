faqs:
  - question: How does a semi-lift valve differ from a pilot operated one?
    answer: A pilot operated valve needs a minimum pressure difference to open. A semi-lift valve does not, because its plunger physically pulls the diaphragm up. The trade-off is a stronger coil for the same size of valve.
  - question: Can the general version go on hot water or steam?
    answer: No. Steam and hot water attack ordinary elastomers and run the coil hotter than it is built for. Use the steam version, and fit it with the coil on top where possible so condensate does not sit in the plunger tube.
  - question: Does it work on a tank that feeds by gravity?
    answer: Yes, that is one of its main uses. Because it can open with almost no pressure behind it, it works on overhead tank outlets and low-head lines where a plain pilot operated valve would stay shut.

A semi-lift solenoid valve, also called assisted lift, combines the two main solenoid principles. Like a pilot operated valve it has a diaphragm with a pilot port, so line pressure helps to open it. Unlike one, its plunger is coupled to the diaphragm, so when the coil pulls in it also lifts the diaphragm off the seat directly.

That coupling lets the valve open even when there is no pressure difference across it. The pilot assistance, in turn, lets it pass far more flow than a direct acting valve with a coil of the same size.

The general version uses elastomer diaphragms and seals suited to water, air and light oils. The steam version uses diaphragm and seal compounds and a coil made for higher temperatures. Always state which one you need: a general valve on steam soon fails.

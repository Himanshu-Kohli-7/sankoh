faqs:
  - question: Why will my pilot operated valve not open at low pressure?
    answer: It relies on a pressure difference between inlet and outlet to lift the diaphragm. If the supply is too weak, such as a tank feeding by gravity, the diaphragm stays down however the coil is switched. A semi-lift or direct acting valve is the answer on lines like that.
  - question: Does it close more gently than a direct acting valve?
    answer: Yes. The chamber above the diaphragm has to refill through the bleed hole before the valve seats, so it shuts over a short moment rather than instantly. That softer closing helps to limit water hammer on longer water lines.
  - question: Does it matter which way round it is fitted?
    answer: It does. The body carries a flow arrow, and fitted backwards the pilot circuit cannot work. A strainer upstream is worth adding too, because grit can block the small bleed and pilot holes and leave the valve stuck open or shut.
  - question: Which parts wear out first?
    answer: Usually the diaphragm, and now and then the plunger seal. Both can be replaced, so a valve that passes when shut or will not close can often be put right without changing the body.

A pilot operated diaphragm solenoid valve lets the pressure already in the line do the heavy lifting. A rubber diaphragm separates the inlet from the outlet, and a small bleed hole lets inlet pressure fill the chamber above it, pressing the diaphragm firmly onto its seat.

When the coil is energised, the plunger uncovers a tiny pilot port that drains the upper chamber to the outlet faster than the bleed hole can refill it. Pressure underneath then lifts the diaphragm and the main flow passes. Switch the coil off, the pilot closes, the chamber refills and the valve shuts.

Because it needs a minimum pressure difference to work, this valve suits pumped and mains-fed lines rather than gravity tanks. For lines that can drop to almost nothing, look at the [semi-lift diaphragm valve](/products/solenoid-valves/semi-lift-diaphragm-solenoid-valve/).

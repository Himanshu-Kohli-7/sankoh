  - question: Why do solenoid coils burn out?
    answer: Common causes are the wrong supply voltage, a sticking plunger that stops an AC coil pulling in fully, heat from steam or hot media, and moisture getting into the connector. Find the cause before fitting a new coil, or the replacement may fail the same way.

The coil is the electrical half of a solenoid valve: a winding of copper wire, usually sealed in moulded resin, that slides over the valve's plunger tube. When current flows, it creates a magnetic field that pulls the plunger in and opens or closes the valve. The coil never touches the media, so it can be changed without opening the line.

When you replace one, three things must match. The coil's bore and height must fit the plunger tube. Its voltage, and whether it is AC or DC, must suit the supply. And its insulation class must suit the heat coming up from the line. A coil on the wrong voltage will either fail to pull in or overheat.

Coils connect by a plug-in connector, flying leads or a terminal box. Matching the original connection makes the swap a few minutes' work with no rewiring.

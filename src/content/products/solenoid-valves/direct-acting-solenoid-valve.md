faqs:
  - question: What do 2/2 way and 3/2 way mean?
    answer: The first number counts the ports and the second counts the switching positions. A 2/2 way valve simply opens or closes one line. A 3/2 way valve connects its outlet either to the supply or to an exhaust, which is what a single-acting cylinder needs to extend and then retract.
  - question: When should I choose direct acting over pilot operated?
    answer: When the line may have little or no pressure, such as a tank outlet fed by gravity, or for small ports where a quick, positive switch matters most. Pilot operated valves pass larger flows, but they will not work without some pressure difference across them.
  - question: Normally closed or normally open?
    answer: Think about the power cut first. A normally closed valve shuts when its coil is de-energised and a normally open one stays open. Choose the form that leaves the process safe, then check which state the valve spends most of its time in, because the coil warms up while it is powered.
  - question: Can it drive a double-acting actuator?
    answer: A 3/2 way valve suits spring-return actuators. A double-acting actuator needs air switched to both sides in turn, which is normally the job of a 5/2 way valve. Tell us the actuator type and we will recommend the right one.

In a direct acting solenoid valve the magnetic pull of the coil does all the work. When the coil is energised, it draws a soft-iron plunger up its tube and the seal on the plunger's tip lifts clear of the orifice. When the power goes off, a spring pushes the plunger back onto the seat and the flow stops.

Nothing in that action depends on line pressure, so the valve opens and closes from zero upward. The limit is size: a coil can only pull against so much force, which is why direct acting valves suit small orifices and modest flows.

A 2/2 way valve has two ports and two positions, on or off. A 3/2 way valve adds an exhaust port, so it can pressurise a spring-return [pneumatic actuator](/products/pneumatic-actuated-valves/) or cylinder and then vent it to let the spring return.

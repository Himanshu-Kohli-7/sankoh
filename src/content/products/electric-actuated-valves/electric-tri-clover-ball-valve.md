  - question: Why use an electric rather than a pneumatic TC valve?
    answer: Many milk ATMs, collection centres and small plants have a power supply but no compressor, and adding one brings noise, oil and moisture to manage. An electric tri-clover ball valve is a sanitary ball valve whose ends take tri-clover (TC) clamps, worked by an electric motor actuator rather than by hand. It brings automatic control to milk, food and pharmaceutical lines in places where there is a power socket but no compressed air, such as a milk ATM or a village collection centre.

Inside the polished stainless body, a ball with a straight bore turns a quarter turn between soft seats. Open, the bore forms a smooth, uninterrupted passage that drains and flushes easily. Each end finishes in a grooved ferrule that joins the next pipe with a gasket and a hinged clamp. Undoing two clamps lets the valve come out of the line in minutes.

The actuator drives the ball through a gearbox and stops it at each end of travel. If the power fails, the line can still be opened or closed with the handwheel, and the clear dome above the actuator shows which way the ball is set. For extra feedback and local control units, see [valve automation accessories](/products/valve-automation-accessories/). Sanitary [solenoid valves](/products/solenoid-valves/) are another option for very small dispensing lines.

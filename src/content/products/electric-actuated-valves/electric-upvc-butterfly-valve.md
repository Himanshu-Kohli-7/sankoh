faqs:
  - question: Why does the actuator size matter so much on a plastic valve?
    answer: A plastic stem and disc take less twisting force than steel ones. An actuator far bigger than the valve needs can shear the stem if the disc jams, so it should be matched to the valve, with its torque or limit settings left as set.
  - question: When is a UPVC butterfly valve better than a UPVC ball valve?
    answer: On the larger sizes. A plastic ball valve becomes long, heavy and expensive as the bore grows, while the butterfly body stays slim. For small lines and tight shut-off, the ball valve is usually preferred.
  - question: Can the valve be left part-open to control flow?
    answer: Yes, with a modulating actuator that follows a signal from a flow or level controller. Keep the working range away from the nearly closed position, where fast-moving water erodes the seat and control becomes jumpy.

An electric UPVC butterfly valve combines a slim butterfly valve moulded from UPVC, a rigid plastic that does not rust or scale, with an electric actuator. It brings motorised control to large plastic pipelines carrying treated water, effluent or chemicals, in plants with electricity but no air compressor.

The body clamps between two plastic flanges. Its disc rotates 90 degrees on a stem inside a rubber seat, edge-on to open and square across the bore to close. Short and light, it suits plastic pipework, which sags or cracks under a heavy metal valve.

The actuator bolts to the valve's top flange through a coupling. Its gearing turns and holds the disc, and switches cut the motor at the ends of travel. A handwheel and dome indicator sit on the housing. [Valve automation accessories](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) cover feedback. With instrument air on site, a pneumatic valve worked through a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) acts faster.

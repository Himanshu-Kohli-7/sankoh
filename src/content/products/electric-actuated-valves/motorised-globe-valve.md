A motorised globe valve is a globe valve with an electric actuator that drives its stem up and down, so it can hold any position between shut and fully open. It is the electric counterpart of the air-operated control valve, and the usual way to regulate heating and cooling water in buildings that have no compressed air.

Flow passes through the body via an opening called the seat. A plug on the end of the stem moves towards or away from that opening, and the gap between the two sets how much passes. Because the plug moves straight along the line of the flow rather than swinging across it, a globe valve gives fine, stable regulation across most of its travel.

The actuator converts its motor rotation into a straight push and pull on the stem. A controller, room thermostat or building management system sends it a signal, and the actuator positions the valve to match, often reporting its actual position back. Most have a manual knob or handwheel for setting the valve by hand. [Position feedback units](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) add signals for the panel, and on/off steam or water duties on small lines may suit a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/).

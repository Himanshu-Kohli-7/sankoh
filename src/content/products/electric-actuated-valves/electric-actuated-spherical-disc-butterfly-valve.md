  - question: What does the spherical disc change in practice?
    answer: A plain disc is pushed edge-first into its seat, and the rubber takes a scrubbing each time. A disc whose rim follows a spherical curve meets the seat with an even pressure all round, so it shuts off more consistently and the seat lasts longer between overhauls.
  - question: Why pair this valve with an electric actuator rather than an air one?
    answer: Usually because the site has no compressed air at that point, or because the valve has to talk to a building management system or PLC over the same wiring as other electrical equipment. The motorised drive also closes the valve gently, which suits a disc that relies on an even seat contact.
  - question: How often can an electric actuator cycle?

An electric actuated spherical disc butterfly valve is a butterfly valve built for a closer seal than the standard design, with a motorised drive in place of a lever or gearbox. It keeps the short butterfly body but shuts off more tightly.

The difference is the disc. Its rim is machined to a section of a sphere, so as the disc swings closed it rolls evenly onto the seat all the way round, instead of being forced in at the sides first. Contact is more uniform and seat wear lower over thousands of cycles.

The actuator sits on a bracket above the valve. A motor and gear train turn the stem a quarter turn, and switches stop it at each end. A handwheel and indicator are built in. Modulating actuators and [position feedback units](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) let it regulate as well as isolate. Small branch lines may suit a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/).

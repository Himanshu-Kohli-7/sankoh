faqs:
  - question: What happens to the valve if the power goes off?
    answer: A standard electric actuator stops and holds wherever it was, because its gearing does not run backwards. The valve can then be moved with the handwheel. If the line must shut or open on its own during a power cut, ask for a fail-safe actuator with a spring or battery backup.
  - question: Which supply voltage do I need?
    answer: Electric actuators are wound for specific supplies, with single-phase AC, three-phase AC and low-voltage DC versions all common. Tell us what is available at the valve and we will offer an actuator to match. Never connect one to a supply other than the one on its label.
  - question: How does the panel know the valve has opened?
    answer: Cams inside the actuator trip limit switches at each end of travel. One set stops the motor, and spare contacts can be wired back to the panel as open and closed signals. The dome indicator on top shows the same thing to anyone standing at the valve.

An electric actuated ball valve, also sold as a motorised ball valve, carries an electric actuator in the place a hand lever would normally go. The valve can then be opened and shut by an electrical signal from a panel, PLC, timer or level controller, with no compressed air needed anywhere on site.

Inside the body sits a ball with a round bore through it. A quarter turn lines the bore up with the pipe to open the line, and another quarter turn back puts the solid side of the ball across it to shut. The actuator gives that turn with a small motor driving the stem through a reduction gearbox. Cams on its output shaft switch the motor off at fully open and fully closed.

The valve shown has a three-piece body with screwed ends. Its centre section can be swung out for new seats while the end pieces stay on the pipe. A handwheel turns the valve by hand during a power cut, and the dome on top shows its position. For feedback and local control options, see [valve automation accessories](/products/valve-automation-accessories/). Small lines that only switch clean water or air are sometimes better served by a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/).

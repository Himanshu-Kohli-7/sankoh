faqs:
  - question: What happens to the valve if the power goes off?
    answer: A standard electric actuator stops and holds wherever it was, because its gearing does not run backwards. The valve can then be moved with the handwheel. If the line must shut or open on its own during a power cut, ask for a fail-safe actuator with a spring or battery backup.
  - question: Which supply voltage do I need?
    answer: Electric actuators are wound for specific supplies, with single-phase AC, three-phase AC and low-voltage DC versions all common. Tell us what is available at the valve and we will offer an actuator to match. Never connect one to a supply other than the one on its label.
  - question: How does the panel know the valve has opened?
    answer: Cams inside the actuator trip limit switches at each end of travel. One set stops the motor, and spare contacts can be wired back to the panel as open and closed signals. The dome indicator on top shows the same thing to anyone standing at the valve.

An electric actuated ball valve, also sold as a motorised ball valve, carries an electric actuator where a hand lever would normally go. A panel, PLC, timer or level controller can then open and shut the line, with no compressed air needed on site.

Inside, a bored ball rotates 90 degrees: bore aligned with the pipe to open, crosswise to shut. A small motor drives the stem through a reduction gearbox, and cams on the output shaft switch the motor off at each end of travel.

The valve shown has a three-piece body with screwed ends, so its centre section swings out for new seats while the ends stay on the pipe. A handwheel works it during a power cut, and the dome on top shows its position. Feedback options are under [valve automation accessories](/products/valve-automation-accessories/). Small clean lines may suit a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/).

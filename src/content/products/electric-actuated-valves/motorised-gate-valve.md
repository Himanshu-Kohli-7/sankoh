faqs:
  - question: How is a multi-turn actuator different from a quarter-turn one?
    answer: A quarter-turn actuator moves its output through 90 degrees and stops. A multi-turn actuator keeps turning for as many revolutions as the valve stem needs, often dozens on a large gate valve, counting them so it knows where the travel ends.
  - question: What stops the actuator from damaging the valve?
    answer: Two kinds of protection. Position limits stop the motor at the set open and closed points. Torque switching cuts the motor if resistance rises sharply, for instance when the gate is fully seated or something is jammed in the bore. Both are set during commissioning.
  - question: How long does it take to open?
    answer: Usually a minute or more on large valves, depending on the stem travel and the actuator speed. That slow movement is useful on long water mains, because it keeps pressure surges down when pumps start and stop.
  - question: Do you overhaul motorised gate valves?
    answer: Yes. Seats, wedges, stems and packing are renewed on motorised gate valves of any make, and the electric actuator is overhauled alongside. Workshop work is typically turned round in 2–3 days.

A motorised gate valve is a gate valve fitted with a multi-turn electric actuator in place of its handwheel. A gate valve may need dozens of turns to go from shut to open, which is slow and hard work on large sizes, so a motor drive lets operators open or close big lines from a panel or SCADA screen instead of sending someone out to wind the valve.

The valve itself works by lifting a wedge or flat gate out of the flow path. Fully open, the bore is clear, with almost no loss of pressure. Fully shut, the gate seats between two faces and isolates the line. It is an isolation valve and should not be parked part-way open to regulate flow, because the gate then vibrates and wears.

The actuator mounts on top of the bonnet and turns the stem nut through a gearbox. It counts turns to find its end positions and also watches the motor torque, so it stops safely when the gate seats or meets an obstruction. A handwheel declutches for manual use. For position signals and local or remote control stations, see [valve automation accessories](/products/valve-automation-accessories/). Smaller automated isolation is often done with a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) or quarter-turn valve instead.

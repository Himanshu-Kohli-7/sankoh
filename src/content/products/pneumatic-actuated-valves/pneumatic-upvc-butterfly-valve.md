faqs:
  - question: Why choose a UPVC butterfly valve over a metal one?
    answer: It does not corrode in treated water, effluent or many chemicals, it is much lighter, and it matches the plastic flanges on UPVC pipework. A metal valve is the better choice where the line runs hot, at higher pressure, or carries media that attack plastics.
  - question: Does the pipework need extra support?
    answer: Plastic pipe is less rigid than steel, and an actuator adds weight above the valve. Support the pipe on both sides of the valve so the actuator does not bend the line or strain the flanges.
  - question: How tight should the flange bolts be?
    answer: Only as tight as needed to seal. The rubber seat of a wafer butterfly valve also acts as the flange gasket, and over-tightening can crack plastic flanges or squeeze the seat so the disc cannot turn. Tighten evenly, in a cross pattern, and check that the valve opens freely afterwards.

A pneumatic UPVC butterfly valve is a wafer butterfly valve with a body made of UPVC, a tough, non-corroding plastic, with an air actuator on top. It opens and closes larger plastic pipelines automatically, where a ball valve of the same bore would be bulky and expensive.

The valve fits between two flanges. A disc on a central stem turns a quarter turn inside a rubber seat: in line with the flow to open, square across it to close. Because the body is light, it suits plastic pipework, which cannot carry the weight of a cast iron or steel valve without extra support.

The actuator bolts to the top of the valve. A [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) switches its air on a command from the plant controls, and a [limit switch box](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) reports the disc position, so an operator can see at the panel which lines are open.

faqs:
  - question: Why use an angle seat valve instead of a steam solenoid valve?
    answer: A solenoid valve opens with an electromagnet, which limits the size and the pressure it can handle and can struggle with dirty media. An angle seat valve uses an air piston, so it can open larger bores against higher pressure and copes better with heavy cycling on steam and hot water.
  - question: What do normally closed and normally open mean?
    answer: They describe what the valve does with no air on the actuator. A normally closed valve is held shut by its spring and opens when air is applied. A normally open valve is the reverse. Double-acting versions use air in both directions and have no spring.
  - question: Does the flow direction matter?
    answer: Yes. The body is marked with an arrow showing which side of the seat the flow should enter. Fitting the valve the wrong way round can stop it sealing properly or cause water hammer when it closes on a liquid line, so tell us the media when you order.
  - question: How is the valve switched from the panel?
    answer: A small solenoid valve, mounted on the actuator or nearby, lets air into the piston on command. A position indicator on top shows whether the valve is open, and some actuators can take a sensor for feedback to the PLC.

A Y-type angle seat valve is an on/off valve driven by a compressed-air piston. In its Y-shaped body the stem and seat sit at an angle to the pipe instead of at right angles. That angle gives the media a smoother, almost straight path through the valve, so more flows for its size than through a globe valve of the same bore.

The actuator sits on top of the angled bonnet. When air enters the cylinder, the piston lifts the stem and disc off the seat. On the common spring-return version, releasing the air lets a spring push the disc back down. The stroke is short, so the valve opens and shuts quickly and is suited to lines that switch many times a day.

Air to the piston is usually switched by a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/), and a [position sensor](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) can be fitted for feedback to the panel.

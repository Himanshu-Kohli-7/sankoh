  - question: How is a power cylinder different from a valve actuator?
    answer: A quarter-turn valve actuator is a compact unit that bolts straight onto the valve. A power cylinder is a straight-line cylinder with a long stroke, mounted on its own frame. Its rod turns the damper shaft through a lever, which gives the large force and travel that big dampers need.

    answer: Yes. With a positioner, the cylinder follows a control signal, usually 4–20 mA, and holds the damper at any opening. This is how draught and combustion air are adjusted on boilers. Without a positioner, it simply drives fully open or fully closed.

A pneumatic power cylinder is a heavy-duty air cylinder that moves a damper instead of a valve. Its piston rod drives a lever clamped to the damper shaft, so the straight stroke of the cylinder becomes the turning movement that opens and closes the damper blades.

Dampers on boilers, fans and large ducts are big and can be stiff, and they often need to be held at a partial opening rather than just open or shut. A power cylinder is built for that: a long stroke and a large piston give plenty of force, and the whole unit sits on its own frame beside the duct.

For modulating duty, a [positioner](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) takes the control signal and sets the cylinder to the matching position, with gauges showing the air pressures. A position indicator box reports the damper position, and a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) can be used for simple open/close service.

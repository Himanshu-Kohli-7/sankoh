faqs:
  - question: What does the positioner on a control valve do?
    answer: It compares the control signal, usually 4–20 mA from the controller or PLC, with the actual position of the valve stem, and adjusts the air to the actuator until the two agree. That makes the valve settle where it is asked to, despite friction in the packing or changing line pressure.
  - question: Should the valve be air-to-open or air-to-close?
    answer: Decide what is safe if the air fails. A valve feeding steam to a heater is usually set to close on air failure, so the process cannot overheat. A valve on a cooling water line is usually set to open, so cooling continues. The actuator spring is arranged to give that fail position.
  - question: What do you need to size a control valve?
    answer: The media, the minimum, normal and maximum flow, the pressure before and after the valve at those flows, and the temperature. From these the required flow capacity is worked out, which often gives a valve smaller than the pipe it sits in.

A pneumatic globe control valve is a globe valve fitted with an air-operated actuator so that it can hold any position between closed and fully open. It is the standard choice where a controller adjusts the valve continuously to keep flow, pressure, level or temperature at a set point.

Inside the body, a plug on the end of a stem moves up and down against a seat. The gap between them sets how much passes through, and the shape of the plug decides how flow changes with travel. The stem is pushed by a diaphragm actuator: air pressure on the diaphragm works against a spring, and the balance between them sets the stem position.

A [positioner](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) takes the control signal and makes sure the valve actually reaches the position asked for. Where a valve only needs to open and close, a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) can switch the actuator air instead.

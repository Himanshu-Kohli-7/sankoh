faqs:
  - question: What does true union mean?
    answer: The valve body is held between two union nuts, each screwed onto an end piece that is joined to the pipe. Unscrew both nuts and the body lifts straight out, so seals can be changed or the valve swapped without cutting the pipe or disturbing the end connections.
  - question: Can a UPVC valve be used on hot water or compressed air?
    answer: Not as a rule. UPVC loses strength as it gets warmer, so it suits cold and moderate temperatures only, and it is not a material for steam. Plastic is also not recommended for compressed air, because a failure under air pressure is sudden. For those lines, choose a metal-bodied actuated valve.

A pneumatic UPVC ball valve combines a ball valve moulded in unplasticised PVC, a rigid plastic that does not rust, with an air-driven actuator that opens and shuts it on command. It is made for plastic pipework carrying treated water and corrosive liquids, where a metal valve would corrode or contaminate the media.

Inside, a ball with a hole through its centre rotates a quarter turn between soft seats to open or shut the line. The body is held between two union nuts. That true-union design lets the whole valve, actuator included, be removed from the line without cutting the pipe.

The actuator is mounted on a bracket above the body. Its air is switched by a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) in response to a level switch, timer or PLC. A [limit switch box](/products/valve-automation-accessories/) on top signals whether the valve is open or closed.

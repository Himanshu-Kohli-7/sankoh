faqs:
  - question: Do I need a quarter-turn or a multi-turn actuator?
    answer: It follows the valve. Ball, butterfly and plug valves turn 90° and take a quarter-turn actuator. Gate and globe valves have a threaded stem that needs many turns, so they take a multi-turn actuator, or a linear one on smaller globe valves.
  - question: Can an electric actuator be fitted to a valve I already have?
    answer: Usually. We need the valve type, size, make, the operating torque if known, and a photo of the top flange and stem. A bracket and coupling are then made to suit. Very old or seized valves may need repair first.
  - question: What happens if the power fails?
    answer: A standard electric actuator stays where it is. If the valve must move to a safe position on power loss, choose a spring-return or battery-backed model, or consider a pneumatic actuator. Most units also have a handwheel or manual override.
  - question: What is the difference between on/off and modulating actuators?
    answer: An on/off actuator drives the valve fully open or fully closed. A modulating actuator has a positioner card that moves the valve to any position in proportion to a 4–20 mA or 0–10 V signal, and is built for frequent starts.

An electric actuator turns electrical power into the movement that works a valve. A motor drives a gear train, the gear train turns the output shaft, and switches inside stop the motor when the valve reaches the end of its travel or meets too much resistance.

This page is about the actuator as a unit in its own right, for example to motorise a valve you already have or to replace a failed actuator. It mounts on the valve through a bracket and coupling matched to the valve's top flange and stem. If you want a valve and actuator supplied together as a tested package, see the [electric actuated ball valve](/products/electric-actuated-valves/electric-actuated-ball-valve/) and the other motorised valves.

Sizing starts from the torque or thrust the valve needs, with a margin on top, and then the supply voltage, the speed of operation and the environment. Where compressed air is on hand and fast or fail-safe action matters, a [pneumatic rotary actuator](/products/valve-automation-accessories/pneumatic-rotary-actuator/) is often the simpler answer.

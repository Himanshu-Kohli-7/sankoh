A basic electric actuator opens and closes on command and stops at its limit switches. On many sites that is not enough. The control room wants to see exactly how far open the valve is, and the operator standing beside it wants to drive it without calling the panel. Position feedback and local control units add both.

A feedback unit uses a potentiometer or a position transmitter driven from the actuator's output, and sends a signal that tracks the valve opening, usually a current signal for a PLC or building management system. Extra switches can flag positions part-way through the travel.

A local control unit adds a local/off/remote selector with open, stop and close push buttons, and often indicator lamps. In local mode the panel's commands are locked out, which protects anyone working on the line. Both suit the [electric actuated valves](/products/electric-actuated-valves/) in our range.

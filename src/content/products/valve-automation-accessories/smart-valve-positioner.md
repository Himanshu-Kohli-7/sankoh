A control valve is only as good as its ability to go where it is told. Friction in the packing, changing line pressure and the stiffness of the actuator all push it off its setpoint. A valve positioner closes that gap. It reads the control signal, measures the valve's real position through a feedback link, then raises or lowers the air pressure on the actuator until signal and position agree.

A smart positioner does this with a microprocessor instead of levers, springs and nozzles. It is most often found on a [pneumatic globe control valve](/products/pneumatic-actuated-valves/pneumatic-globe-control-valve/), and on ball and butterfly valves used for throttling. It can calibrate itself at the push of a button, find its own end stops and tuning, and keep a record of valve travel that helps to plan maintenance. Many also send a position signal back to the panel and talk digitally over the same pair of wires.

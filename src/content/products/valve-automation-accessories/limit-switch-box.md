faqs:
  - question: Mechanical switches or proximity sensors?
    answer: Mechanical microswitches are inexpensive and simple to wire. Inductive proximity sensors have no contacts to wear or bounce, so they last longer on valves that cycle often and suit damp or dirty surroundings.
  - question: How are the cams set?
    answer: With the valve driven fully open, the open cam is turned until its switch just changes over, then locked. The same is done at the shut end. Setting each one slightly inside the end of travel gives a dependable signal.
  - question: Will it fit an actuator from a different maker?
    answer: Usually, provided the actuator has the common top shaft and accessory mounting holes. A bracket and coupling adapt it. Send the actuator make and model and we will confirm.
  - question: Why does the PLC show the valve as neither open nor shut?
    answer: Often the valve has stopped short of its end position, a cam has slipped on the shaft, or a wire has worked loose. A sticking valve gives the same symptom, so check the actuator's travel before blaming the switches.

A limit switch box tells the control system what a valve has actually done, not just what it was told to do. It mounts on top of a quarter-turn actuator, and a shaft inside turns with the actuator's output. Two adjustable cams on that shaft trip switches at the open and closed ends of travel.

Each switch sends a signal back to the panel or PLC. The switches may be mechanical contacts, which keep the wiring simple, or proximity sensors, which have no moving contacts to wear. A domed indicator on the lid shows the operator at a glance whether the valve is open or shut.

Paired with a [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) on the actuator, it completes a basic on/off automation package: one command out to the valve, two position signals back.

faqs:
  - question: Why fit one at each valve when the compressor has a dryer?
    answer: Air picks up rust, scale and condensate in the pipework after the dryer. The local unit catches what reaches the actuator, and its regulator holds the pressure steady even when the header pressure swings.
  - question: What filter grade do I need?
    answer: A 40 micron element suits most actuators and cylinders. Positioners and I/P converters have fine air passages and usually call for 5 micron, sometimes with a coalescing filter after it to remove oil mist.
  - question: Manual or automatic drain?
    answer: A manual drain has to be opened regularly, and on a wet air system it is often forgotten until water reaches the actuator. An automatic float drain empties the bowl by itself, which is worth having on valves that are hard to reach.
  - question: What pressure should I set?
    answer: The pressure the actuator or positioner was sized for, usually stated as a range. Setting it higher than needed wears seals and can overstress small valves; setting it too low leaves the valve short of torque.

An air filter regulator, often shortened to AFR, is two jobs in one body. Air enters the filter section first, where a deflector spins it so that water droplets and heavy dirt are thrown against the bowl wall and fall to the bottom. The air then passes through a fine element that catches smaller particles. The regulator above it lets through only enough air to hold the outlet at the pressure set on the knob, whatever the supply pressure does above that.

Pneumatic actuators, cylinders and especially positioners last much longer on clean, dry air at a steady pressure. A gauge shows the set pressure, and a drain at the bottom of the bowl lets the collected water out.

On automated valves the unit is usually mounted on or close to the actuator, ahead of the [NAMUR solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/namur-solenoid-valve/) or the [smart valve positioner](/products/valve-automation-accessories/smart-valve-positioner/).

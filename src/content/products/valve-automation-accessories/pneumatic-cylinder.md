faqs:
  - question: How much force will a cylinder give?
    answer: Multiply the air pressure by the piston area. On the return stroke the rod takes up part of that area, so a double acting cylinder pulls with less force than it pushes. Allow a margin for friction and for the lowest pressure your air supply drops to.
  - question: Single acting or double acting?
    answer: A single acting cylinder uses air for one stroke and a spring for the other, so it returns to a set position when the air is cut, but its stroke is short and the spring costs force. A double acting cylinder is powered both ways and suits long strokes and heavier loads.
  - question: What is cushioning for?
    answer: It slows the piston in the last part of its travel so it does not slam into the end cap. Adjustable air cushions are usual on medium and large bores; small cylinders often have rubber bumpers instead.
  - question: How do I know where the piston is?
    answer: Choose a cylinder with a magnet in the piston. Reed or electronic sensors clipped to the barrel then signal the end positions, or any point in between, to a PLC or relay.

A pneumatic cylinder turns compressed air into straight-line movement. Inside a smooth barrel sits a piston fixed to a rod. Air let into one end drives the piston along and pushes the rod out; air let into the other end pulls it back. A [solenoid valve](/products/solenoid-valves/) switches the air, so a PLC or a simple push button can control the stroke.

The force comes from the air pressure acting on the piston, so a larger bore gives more thrust at the same pressure. The stroke is simply how far the rod travels. End caps carry the air ports and, on most industrial cylinders, adjustable cushions that slow the piston before it reaches the end.

This page covers the cylinder as a separate component, whether for a valve, a gate or a machine. A [pneumatic power cylinder for dampers](/products/pneumatic-actuated-valves/pneumatic-power-cylinder-for-dampers/) is a complete damper drive built around a long-stroke cylinder, with a lever, a frame and usually a positioner. Feed any cylinder through an [air filter regulator](/products/valve-automation-accessories/air-filter-regulator/): dirty or wet air is the main cause of worn seals.

faqs:
  - question: Does a thermowell make the reading slower?
    answer: A little, because heat has to pass through the wall of the well before reaching the sensor. Keeping the bore just large enough for the stem, and filling small gaps with heat-transfer paste, keeps the delay small.
  - question: Why does the thermowell need to suit the flow?
    answer: Fluid flowing past a long, thin well sheds swirls that make it vibrate. If that vibration matches the natural frequency of the well, it can crack and break off. A shorter or stiffer well, chosen with the flow velocity in mind, avoids this.
  - question: Will my existing gauge fit a new thermowell?
    answer: Usually, if the bore suits the stem diameter and the internal thread or fitting matches the gauge. Send us the stem diameter, length and connection of the instrument, or a photo, and we will match the well to it.

A thermowell is a tube, closed at one end, that is screwed, welded or flanged into a pipe or vessel. The temperature gauge or sensor slides into it from outside. The well, not the instrument, takes the pressure, flow and attack from the process.

That separation brings two benefits. The instrument is protected from erosion, corrosion and mechanical damage, so it lasts longer. And it can be withdrawn for checking or replacement while the line stays full and under pressure, with no draining or shutdown.

Wells are made either by drilling out solid bar, which suits higher pressure and velocity, or from tube with a welded tip for lighter duty. The length, bore and outside profile are chosen to suit the instrument, the line size and the speed of the flow.

faqs:
  - question: Which thermocouple type should I use?
    answer: Type K is the general-purpose choice and covers most industrial work up to roughly 1100 °C. Type J is common on plastics machinery and older equipment. Type T suits low temperatures and food duties. Types N, R, S and B are for higher temperatures. Match the type your controller or indicator is set for.
  - question: Should I use a thermocouple or an RTD?
    answer: An RTD is more accurate and stable up to a few hundred degrees and is the usual pick for process liquids. A thermocouple covers far higher temperatures, responds faster in small sizes, is more rugged against vibration and costs less. Above about 500 to 600 °C, a thermocouple is normally the only practical option.
  - question: Why does the cable type matter?
    answer: The signal is only a few millivolts and depends on the metals all the way back to the instrument. Ordinary copper cable adds an error, so the run must be made in extension or compensating cable of the same type, with the polarity kept right.
  - question: What is a grounded or ungrounded junction?
    answer: In a grounded junction the tip is welded to the sheath, which gives the fastest response but can pick up electrical noise. An ungrounded junction is insulated from the sheath, a little slower, and better where electrical isolation matters.

A thermocouple is the workhorse of industrial temperature measurement. It is a pair of wires in two different metals, welded together at the measuring junction. When that junction is hotter or colder than the other end, a small voltage appears, and the instrument reads it as a temperature. Each standard pairing of metals has a letter, such as K or J, and its own voltage curve.

The sensing part is tiny, tough and cheap to make in many forms. Mineral insulated thermocouples pack the wires in compacted powder inside a thin metal sheath, so they can be bent to shape and pushed into tight places. Heavier designs put the wires in a protection tube with a terminal head, for furnaces and process lines.

This page covers thermocouples in general, across the usual temperature range. For kilns and furnaces at the top of the range, where the protection tube and wire grade become the main design question, see the [high temperature sensor](/products/temperature-instruments/high-temperature-sensor/). For the closest accuracy on moderate temperatures, see the [RTD sensor](/products/temperature-instruments/rtd-sensor/).

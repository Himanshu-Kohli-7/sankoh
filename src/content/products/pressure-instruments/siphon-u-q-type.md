A siphon is a short length of tube bent into a loop and fitted between a steam line and its pressure gauge. Steam entering the loop cools and condenses, and the water collects in the bend. That trapped water forms a seal: the pressure passes through it to the gauge, but the heat of the steam does not.

The two common shapes are named after the letters they resemble. A U type siphon has a single U-shaped bend. A Q type, also called a pigtail or coil siphon, has a full circular loop and is more compact.

Without a siphon, steam reaches the Bourdon tube of the gauge directly. The heat weakens the element, ruins its calibration and can crack the soldered or welded joints inside. A siphon costs little and saves gauges, which makes it standard fitting on almost every steam line.

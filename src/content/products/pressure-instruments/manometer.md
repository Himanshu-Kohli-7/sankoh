faqs:
  - question: U-tube, well type or inclined?
    answer: A U-tube is simple and reads the difference between its two limbs directly. A well-type manometer has one large reservoir, so you read a single column. An inclined manometer lays the tube at a shallow angle, which stretches the scale and makes very small pressures, such as duct static or draft, easier to read.
  - question: Why is a manometer still used when digital gauges exist?
    answer: It needs no power, and its reading follows directly from the liquid density and the column height, so it does not drift. That makes it a common reference for checking other low-pressure instruments.
  - question: What liquid does it use?
    answer: Usually water or a coloured gauge oil whose density the scale is marked for. Gauge oil is lighter than water, so it gives a larger movement for the same pressure. Use only the liquid the scale is made for, or the reading will be wrong.
  - question: Can it be used on water lines or high pressure?
    answer: Liquid column manometers are low-pressure instruments, typically up to a few hundred millimetres or a couple of metres of water column. For line pressure use a pressure gauge, and for a continuous signal use a differential pressure transmitter.

A manometer measures pressure by balancing it against a column of liquid. In a U-tube, both limbs hold the same liquid and stand level when both sides see the same pressure. Connect one limb to a duct, filter or burner and the liquid is pushed down on that side and up on the other. The difference in height, read off the scale, is the pressure or the pressure difference.

Because the reading comes straight from the column height, a manometer is very sensitive at low pressures where a [pressure gauge](/products/pressure-instruments/pressure-gauge/) barely moves. That is why it is the usual instrument for draft, duct static pressure, filter drop and the gas pressure at a burner.

The trade-off is range: liquid columns are practical only for low pressures, and they show the reading locally without sending a signal. When a reading has to reach a BMS or PLC, use a [differential pressure transmitter](/products/pressure-instruments/differential-pressure-transmitter/).

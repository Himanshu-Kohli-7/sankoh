A needle valve is a small valve that controls flow with a slender, tapered stem tip, the needle, which fits into a matching seat. A fine thread moves the needle a tiny distance for each turn of the handle, so the gap around it, and with it the flow, can be set very precisely.

Its main use is on instrument lines. Fitted under a pressure gauge or transmitter, it isolates the instrument for removal or calibration. Opened only a little, it damps the pulses that make a gauge pointer flutter.

Needle valves are made for small line sizes, usually with screwed ends. They pass little flow for their size, so they are not used where a line simply has to be opened fully. A [screwed-end ball valve](/products/manual-valves/ball-valve-screwed-end/) does that better.

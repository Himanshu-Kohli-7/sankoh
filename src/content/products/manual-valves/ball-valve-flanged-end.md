  - question: What is the difference between a full bore and a reduced bore ball valve?
    answer: In a full bore valve the hole through the ball is the same size as the pipe, so flow passes with almost no pressure drop. A reduced bore valve has a smaller ball and port. It is lighter and costs less, with a slightly higher pressure drop.
  - question: Can a ball valve be used for throttling?
    answer: It is best kept fully open or fully closed. Left part open, the flow cuts across the seats and wears them, and the valve may then leak when shut. For throttling duty, a globe valve is the better choice.

A ball valve shuts off flow with a ball that has a hole bored through it. A quarter turn of the lever lines the hole up with the pipe to open the valve, or turns it across the pipe to close it. The ball sits between two seats, which press against it and give a tight shut-off. The lever shows the position at a glance. Along the pipe is open and across it is shut.

This version has flanged ends. Each end bolts to a matching pipe flange with a gasket in between, so the valve can be lifted out for service without cutting or unscrewing the pipework.

For smaller threaded lines, see the [screwed-end ball valve](/products/manual-valves/ball-valve-screwed-end/). If the valve has to work from a panel or PLC, see the [pneumatic actuated ball valve](/products/pneumatic-actuated-valves/pneumatic-actuated-ball-valve/).

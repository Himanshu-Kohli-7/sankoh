faqs:
  - question: What is the difference between a pressure reducing valve and a safety valve?
    answer: A pressure reducing valve works all the time, throttling the flow to keep the downstream pressure at its set value. A safety valve stays shut and opens only to release pressure when something has gone wrong. A reducing station normally has both, with the safety valve fitted downstream.
  - question: Should I choose a direct acting or a pilot operated valve?
    answer: Direct acting valves are simple and compact, and suit small flows where a little pressure drift with load is acceptable. Pilot operated valves hold the set pressure more closely over a wide flow range, so they are used on larger steam and water lines.
  - question: Why does the valve need a strainer in front of it?
    answer: The seat and the pilot passages are small, and dirt or pipe scale lodged there makes the valve pass or hunt. A strainer upstream is the cheapest protection, and on steam a separator or trap point ahead of the valve keeps condensate out.
  - question: How is the valve sized?
    answer: By flow, not by pipe size. Give the inlet pressure, the outlet pressure you want and the maximum and minimum flow. A valve picked to match the line size is often too big and will chatter at low load.

A pressure reducing valve, often shortened to PRV, lowers the pressure in a line and keeps it there. The outlet pressure pushes on a diaphragm, piston or bellows, and a spring pushes back. When demand rises and the outlet pressure sags, the spring opens the valve further. When demand falls, the rising pressure closes it again. The balance point is set by turning the adjusting screw on the spring.

In a pilot operated valve a small pilot does the sensing and uses line pressure to move a larger main valve. That gives closer control and far more capacity from the same body size.

On steam, the valve is usually built into a reducing station with an isolation valve and [strainer](/products/manual-valves/strainer/) upstream, pressure gauges on both sides and a [safety valve](/products/manual-valves/safety-valve/) downstream.

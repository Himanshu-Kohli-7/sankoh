faqs:
  - question: Why is a ball float trap preferred on heat exchangers?
    answer: It discharges condensate the moment it forms, at steam temperature, and keeps doing so as the load and pressure change. Nothing backs up into the steam space, so the heating surface stays fully in use and the temperature control stays steady.
  - question: What does the thermostatic air vent do?
    answer: At start-up the equipment is full of air, which would otherwise sit above the water and stop the trap working. A small thermostatic element inside the trap opens when cool to let the air out and closes as steam arrives. A trap with this vent is often called a float and thermostatic, or FT, trap.
  - question: What is steam lock and how is it avoided?
    answer: If the trap is some way from the equipment, steam can fill the pipe between them and hold the trap shut while condensate collects behind it. A steam lock release, which is a small bleed inside the trap, clears it. Say so if your trap will drain a rotating cylinder or a long siphon pipe.
  - question: Can a float trap be damaged by water hammer or frost?
    answer: Yes, both can crush the float or crack the body. Slope the pipework so condensate drains, lag outdoor traps, and drain them if the plant will stand idle in freezing weather.

A ball float steam trap relies on water being far denser than steam. Condensate entering the body raises a hollow stainless steel float. The float is linked to a valve at the bottom of the trap, below the water line, so as the level rises the valve opens and lets condensate out. When less condensate arrives, the float falls and the valve closes down to match.

Because the outlet is always under water, steam cannot escape through it. And because the valve position follows the level, the discharge is continuous and modulating instead of in bursts. Light loads and heavy loads are handled equally well, and so are sudden changes.

The trap must be chosen for the pressure difference it will work against, since that decides the orifice the float can open. It also has to be installed level, in the orientation it was built for. On steam mains and tracing, where loads are small and conditions are rough, the [thermodynamic steam trap](/products/manual-valves/thermodynamic-steam-trap/) is the more usual choice.

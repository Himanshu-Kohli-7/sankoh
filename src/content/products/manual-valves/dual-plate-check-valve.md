  - question: Why does a dual plate check valve close faster than a swing check valve?
    answer: Each half plate is small and light, travels a short distance and is pushed shut by a spring. The plates start to close as the flow slows, so they are usually seated before the flow reverses.

A dual plate check valve is a wafer-style non-return valve with two half-circle plates hinged on a pin across the middle of the body. Forward flow folds the two plates back, like a pair of doors. Springs on the pin push them shut again as the flow slows, so the valve is closed before the flow can reverse.

That early, spring-assisted closing is the main reason to choose it. A check valve that closes late is slammed shut by the returning flow, and the shock travels through the pipework. Two small plates also weigh much less than one large disc, so the valve stays short and light even in big sizes.

It fits between two pipe flanges and takes the place of a much longer swing check valve.

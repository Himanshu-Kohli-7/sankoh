faqs:
  - question: When should I choose a basket strainer over a Y-type strainer?
    answer: Choose a basket strainer when the flow is high, the liquid is dirty, or the pressure drop has to stay low. Its basket has several times the screen area of a Y-type of the same line size, so it runs longer between cleans. A Y-type is more compact, costs less and can be used on steam and gas as well as liquids.
  - question: What is a duplex basket strainer?
    answer: Two basket strainers side by side with a changeover valve between them. Flow runs through one while the other is opened and cleaned, so the line never stops. It is the usual choice for fuel and lube oil systems and continuous cooling water.
  - question: How do I know when the basket needs cleaning?
    answer: Fit a pressure gauge on each side, or a differential pressure gauge or switch across the strainer. A rising pressure drop means the basket is filling. Clean it before the drop gets high enough to starve the pump or collapse the screen.
  - question: Which way round is a basket strainer installed?
    answer: In a horizontal line with the cover on top, so the basket lifts straight out and the dirt stays in it. Follow the flow arrow on the body, and leave headroom above the cover to withdraw the basket.

A basket strainer, also called a pot strainer or T-type strainer, protects pumps, meters and valves from solids in a liquid line. The liquid enters the body, drops into a perforated basket and passes out through its walls, leaving the debris inside. To clean it, the line is isolated, the top cover is opened and the basket is lifted out, emptied and put back.

What sets it apart is capacity. The basket is deep and wide, so its open area is many times the cross-section of the pipe. Flow slows down inside the body, the pressure drop is small, and a lot of dirt can collect before anything needs attention. That makes it the right choice on pump suctions, where every bit of pressure loss counts, and on dirty water or oil.

It is a liquid strainer and is fitted in horizontal pipework. For steam and gas lines, and for smaller clean services, the more compact Y-type on our [strainer](/products/manual-valves/strainer/) page is the usual pick.

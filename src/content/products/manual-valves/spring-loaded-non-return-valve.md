A spring loaded non-return valve is a check valve whose disc is held on its seat by a spring. Flow in the right direction has to overcome the spring to push the disc open. The moment the flow weakens, the spring pushes the disc back, so the valve is shut before any reverse flow begins.

Because a spring does the closing, not gravity, the valve works in horizontal or vertical lines and closes quietly, without the slam of a swing check valve. The disc travels only a short distance, which keeps the valve compact. Many are thin wafer bodies that fit between two flanges. Others have screwed ends for small lines.

The spring adds a little resistance, so the valve needs a minimum pressure, called the cracking pressure, before it opens. Where the fluid carries debris, fit a [strainer](/products/manual-valves/strainer/) upstream.

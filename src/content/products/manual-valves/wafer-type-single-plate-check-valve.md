  - question: How is a wafer check valve installed?
    answer: It sits between two pipe flanges and is held by the flange bolts, which pass around the outside of the body. Many have an eye bolt to help lift and centre the valve. The arrow on the body must point in the direction of flow.
  - question: What is the difference between a single plate and a dual plate check valve?
    answer: A single plate valve has one hinged plate that swings open into the downstream pipe. A dual plate valve has two half plates on a central pin, closed by springs. The single plate design is simpler. The dual plate design closes faster and is often preferred on larger sizes.

    answer: Yes. The plate swings out beyond the body as it opens, so it needs free space in the pipe downstream. Fitted directly against another valve or fitting, the plate may not open fully.

A wafer type single plate check valve is a slim non-return valve with one hinged plate inside a short, ring-shaped body. Forward flow swings the plate open. When the flow stops or reverses, the plate falls back onto its seat and seals the line. Nothing has to be operated by hand.

The body has no flanges of its own. It is clamped between two pipe flanges by the flange bolts, so it takes up a fraction of the length and weight of a [flanged check valve](/products/manual-valves/non-return-valve-flanged-end/). That makes it a practical choice at pump outlets and in crowded pipework.

The trade-off is access. The valve has no cover, so it has to come out of the line to be inspected. Where faster closing is needed, see the [dual plate check valve](/products/manual-valves/dual-plate-check-valve/).

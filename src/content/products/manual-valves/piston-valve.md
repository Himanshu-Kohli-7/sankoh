faqs:
  - question: What is the difference between a piston valve and a globe valve?
    answer: They look alike from outside and both use a handwheel. A globe valve seals by pressing a disc onto a seat, and that seat can be cut by dirt or by steam passing at high speed. A piston valve seals around the side of a piston with two rings, so the sealing surfaces are wiped clean each time and are far less exposed to wear.
  - question: Can the sealing rings be replaced?
    answer: Yes. The rings and the piston can generally be replaced with the valve body left in the line, which is one reason piston valves are popular with maintenance teams.
  - question: Can a piston valve regulate flow?
    answer: It is mainly a shut-off valve. Some designs have a regulating piston for throttling duty, so tell us if you need to control flow as well as isolate.

A piston valve is a shut-off valve that seals with a smooth cylindrical piston instead of a disc and seat. Two resilient sealing rings sit in the body, one above and one below the flow port, held apart by a metal spacer. Turning the handwheel pushes the piston down through both rings. The lower ring shuts the line and the upper ring seals the stem.

Because the seal is made along the side of the piston, a scratch or a speck of dirt does little harm, and the piston wipes the rings clean every time the valve is worked. That gives a tight, lasting shut-off on steam and other hot services, where an ordinary [globe valve](/products/manual-valves/globe-valve/) can start to pass.

The rings wear in time and can be replaced, which gives the valve a long working life.

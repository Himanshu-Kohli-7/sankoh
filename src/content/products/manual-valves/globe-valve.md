A globe valve regulates flow by moving a disc, or plug, towards or away from a seat set across the inside of the body. Turning the handwheel drives the stem down and the disc closes onto the seat. The opening grows in step with each turn, so the flow can be set finely and held there.

That makes the globe valve the standard hand valve for throttling, and it also shuts off tightly. Its disc and seat stand up to being left part open far better than those of a gate or ball valve.

The cost is pressure drop, because the flow has to turn through the seat instead of passing straight through. For plain isolation, a [gate valve](/products/manual-valves/gate-valve/) wastes less pressure. For very small flows, see the [needle valve](/products/manual-valves/needle-valve/).

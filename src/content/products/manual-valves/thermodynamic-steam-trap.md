faqs:
  - question: Where is a thermodynamic trap the right choice?
    answer: On steam mains, tracing and other duties with a small, fairly steady condensate load at medium to high pressure. It is compact, works over a wide pressure range without adjustment and is not damaged by superheat, water hammer or freezing.
  - question: Why is my thermodynamic trap clicking rapidly?
    answer: A healthy trap clicks open and shut a few times a minute. Rapid, continuous chattering usually means the disc or seat is worn, or dirt is stopping the disc seating, and live steam is being lost. Clean or replace the disc and seat, and fit a strainer if there is none.
  - question: Is it suitable for a heat exchanger or a jacketed pan?
    answer: Generally not. It discharges in bursts and handles air poorly at start-up, so process equipment that needs steady drainage is better served by a ball float trap.
  - question: Does back pressure affect it?
    answer: Yes. If the pressure in the condensate return rises too close to the inlet pressure, the disc cannot close properly. Keep the back pressure well below the inlet pressure, and tell us both when you enquire.

A thermodynamic steam trap, or TD trap, has just one moving part: a hardened disc sitting on a flat seat under a cap. Cool condensate and air lift the disc and flow out. When hot condensate arrives it flashes into steam as it passes under the disc. The fast-moving flash steam lowers the pressure beneath the disc and builds pressure in the chamber above it, and the disc snaps down onto the seat.

The trap stays shut until the steam trapped in the cap chamber cools and condenses. The pressure above the disc then falls, the disc lifts, and the cycle repeats. That on-off action is what gives the trap its characteristic click.

With so little inside it, the trap is small, robust and easy to check by ear. It can be mounted in any position, though horizontal with the cap on top is preferred. Its natural partner on process equipment is the [ball float steam trap](/products/manual-valves/ball-float-steam-trap/), and the [steam traps](/products/manual-valves/steam-traps/) page compares all the common types.

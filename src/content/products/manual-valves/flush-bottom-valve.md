  - question: Why not use an ordinary valve on a tank outlet?
    answer: An ordinary valve sits a short distance below the tank on a nozzle, and that length of pipe forms a pocket above the valve. Product settles there, does not mix with the rest of the batch and can block the outlet. A flush bottom valve seals at the vessel wall, so there is no pocket.

    answer: In one design the disc lifts up into the vessel to open, which breaks through any crust or settled solids. In the other the disc moves down into the valve body, which suits vessels where an agitator sweeps close to the bottom. Tell us which your vessel needs.

A flush bottom valve, also called a tank bottom valve, is fitted to the outlet at the lowest point of a tank, reactor or vessel. Its disc seals level with the inside of the vessel bottom. An ordinary valve on the same nozzle would leave a short pocket of pipe above its seat, where product collects, stays unmixed and can set hard.

Most are Y-shaped. The inlet flange bolts to the vessel nozzle, the outlet runs off at an angle and the stem passes down through the body to a handwheel. Turning the handwheel moves the disc off its seat and the contents drain out. Depending on the design, the disc opens upward into the vessel or downward into the valve.

With no dead space above the seat, the whole batch is mixed and the vessel empties fully.

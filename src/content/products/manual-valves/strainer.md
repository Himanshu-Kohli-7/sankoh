faqs:
  - question: What is the difference between a Y-type and a basket strainer?
    answer: A Y-type strainer is compact, holds a modest amount of dirt and can be fitted in horizontal or vertical lines. It is the common choice on steam, air and gas. A basket strainer has a larger chamber and a lift-out basket, so it holds more and suits liquids with heavier dirt loads.
  - question: What mesh size do I need?
    answer: The screen should be fine enough to stop particles that would harm the equipment downstream, and no finer. A finer screen blocks sooner and adds pressure drop. Tell us what the strainer is protecting and our engineers will suggest a screen.
  - question: How often should a strainer be cleaned?
    answer: It depends on how dirty the line is. A rising pressure drop across the strainer is the sign that the screen is filling up. New pipework usually needs the screen cleaned several times in its first weeks.
  - question: Which way round is a Y-strainer installed?
    answer: With the arrow in the direction of flow. On liquid lines the screen pocket points down. On steam and gas lines it is usually turned to the side, so that condensate does not collect in it.

A strainer is a pipeline fitting that catches solid particles before they reach equipment they could damage. The flow passes through a perforated or mesh screen inside the body. Scale, rust, weld beads and other debris stay behind in the screen, and clean fluid carries on down the line.

The screen is removable. Take off the cap or cover, lift the screen out, clean it and put it back. Many strainers also have a drain plug, so that loose dirt can be flushed out without opening the body.

The two common shapes are the Y-type, which is compact and suits most lines, and the basket type, which holds more dirt. A strainer costs little next to the valve seat, pump or meter it protects.

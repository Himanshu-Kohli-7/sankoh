  - question: Can a gate valve be used to regulate flow?
    answer: No. It is an isolation valve. Left part open, the flow makes the gate vibrate and wears the gate and seats, and the valve may then pass when shut. Use a globe valve to regulate flow.
  - question: What is the difference between a rising stem and a non-rising stem gate valve?
    answer: On a rising stem valve the stem moves up as the valve opens, so the position can be seen from a distance, but it needs headroom. On a non-rising stem valve the stem turns without moving up, which suits tight spaces and underground service.
  - question: Should I use a gate valve or a ball valve for isolation?
    answer: Both isolate a line. A ball valve is quicker, with a quarter turn, and is common on smaller lines. A gate valve opens and closes slowly, which avoids water hammer, and is widely used on larger lines and on steam.

A gate valve isolates a line by sliding a flat or wedge-shaped gate across the flow path. Turning the handwheel moves the gate on a threaded stem. Fully raised, the gate sits clear of the bore, so fluid passes straight through with very little pressure drop. Fully lowered, it wedges between two seats and shuts the line.

It takes many turns to open or close. That is slow, but it means the flow changes gradually, which protects the pipework from water hammer.

A gate valve is meant to be fully open or fully closed. It is the standard choice where a line is isolated only now and then and an unrestricted bore matters. To regulate flow, use a [globe valve](/products/manual-valves/globe-valve/). For slurries and liquids that carry solids, see the [knife gate valve](/products/manual-valves/knife-gate-valve/).

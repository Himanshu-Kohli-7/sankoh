faqs:
  - question: Why does a centrifugal pump need a foot valve?
    answer: A centrifugal pump cannot pull water up an empty suction pipe. The foot valve closes when the pump stops and traps the water in the pipe and casing, so the pump is still primed for the next start. Self-priming and submersible pumps do not need one.
  - question: Why does my pump lose its prime overnight?
    answer: The usual cause is a foot valve that no longer seals, because grit is holding the disc off its seat or the seat has worn. A leaking suction joint gives the same symptom. Lift and clean the valve first, and replace it if the seat is damaged.
  - question: How should a foot valve be installed?
    answer: Vertically at the lowest end of the suction pipe, fully submerged and clear of the sump floor so it does not draw in silt. Keep the strainer open area several times the pipe area to limit suction loss.

A foot valve is a non-return valve made for one position: the foot of a pump suction line. Water rising to the pump lifts the disc or ball and passes through. When the pump stops and the water tries to fall back, the disc drops onto its seat and holds the column in the pipe.

A strainer basket around the inlet stops leaves, stones and rags reaching the valve seat and the pump impeller. Because the whole assembly sits under water at the bottom of a sump, it is out of sight, so a dependable seat and a strainer that does not clog easily matter more than anything else.

Every bit of resistance on the suction side reduces what the pump can lift, so foot valves are made with a generous flow area. For check valves elsewhere in the system, see the [flanged non-return valve](/products/manual-valves/non-return-valve-flanged-end/).

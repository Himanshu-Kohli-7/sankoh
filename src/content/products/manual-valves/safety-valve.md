faqs:
  - question: What is the set pressure of a safety valve?
    answer: It is the pressure at which the valve starts to open. It is chosen above the normal working pressure of the system and no higher than the pressure the protected equipment is designed for.
  - question: What is the difference between a safety valve and a relief valve?
    answer: The names are often used loosely. Strictly, a safety valve pops fully open and is used on steam, air and gases. A relief valve opens gradually, in proportion to the over-pressure, and is used on liquids.
  - question: What do you need to know to size a safety valve?
    answer: The media, the set pressure, the working temperature and the flow the valve has to discharge, along with the connection sizes. A safety valve that is too small cannot protect the equipment, so sizing matters more here than for most valves.
  - question: Can an isolation valve be fitted before a safety valve?
    answer: As a rule, no. Nothing should be able to shut a safety valve off from the equipment it protects. Follow the regulations that apply to your boiler or vessel.

A safety valve protects a boiler, vessel or pipeline from damage by too much pressure. Inside, a spring holds a disc down on its seat. While the system pressure stays below the valve's set pressure, the valve is shut. When the pressure rises to the set point, it overcomes the spring, the disc lifts and the excess is discharged. Once the pressure has fallen, the spring closes the valve again.

It works entirely on its own, with no operator, air or power, which is what makes it the last line of defence when other controls fail.

Each safety valve has to be chosen for its duty. The set pressure, the media and the amount it must discharge decide the size and type, so we need those details to offer the right valve.

A BOD sensor gives a continuous estimate of biochemical oxygen demand: how much oxygen bacteria would use to break down the organic matter in wastewater. BOD is a key measure of how polluting an effluent is and how hard a treatment plant has to work.

The standard BOD test is done in a laboratory and takes several days, so its result describes water that has long since passed through the plant. An online sensor closes that gap. Most work optically: they shine ultraviolet and visible light through a small gap filled with sample and measure how much is absorbed. Organic compounds absorb ultraviolet light, so the absorption tracks the organic load. Turbidity is measured too and corrected for.

The reading is related to BOD through a correlation built from lab results on the same wastewater. Probes are immersed in a channel or tank, or fitted to a flow cell.

faqs:
  - question: What is the difference between free and total chlorine?
    answer: Free chlorine is the active disinfectant, present as hypochlorous acid and hypochlorite. Some chlorine reacts with ammonia and organic matter to form combined chlorine, which disinfects far more weakly. Total chlorine is the two added together. Drinking water control is usually on free chlorine and effluent limits on total.
  - question: Why does pH affect the chlorine reading?
    answer: The balance between hypochlorous acid and hypochlorite moves with pH, and many sensors respond mainly to the acid form. If the pH of the sample varies, choose an analyzer with pH compensation or a buffered measuring cell.
  - question: Amperometric or colorimetric, which should I use?
    answer: An amperometric sensor reads continuously, uses no reagents and suits control loops, but needs a steady sample flow and periodic calibration. A colorimetric (DPD) analyzer runs a reagent test every few minutes, is less affected by the sample's make-up and is often chosen where readings are reported to a regulator.
  - question: How is the analyzer kept accurate?
    answer: Check it against a hand-held DPD test on the same sample and adjust if needed, renew the membrane cap and electrolyte on membrane sensors as they age, and keep the flow through the cell steady.

Chlorine is added to water to kill bacteria, and a small amount must remain afterwards to keep the water safe on its way to the user. That remainder is the residual. Too little and the water is unprotected; too much and it tastes and smells, corrodes equipment and wastes chemical. A chlorine analyzer measures the residual all the time so the dosing can be held at the right level.

Most online analyzers use an amperometric sensor. Chlorine reaching the sensor's electrode is reduced there, and the tiny current this produces is in proportion to the concentration. The sensor sits in a flow cell fed by a steady trickle of sample, and a transmitter shows the reading in mg/L and passes it to the dosing system.

Where chlorine has to be removed, not added, the same analyzer proves it has gone, which matters ahead of RO membranes. For a simpler signal that shows whether water is oxidising without giving a concentration, see the [ORP analyzer](/products/analytical-instruments/orp-analyzer/).

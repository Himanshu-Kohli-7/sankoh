faqs:
  - question: Why is DO control so important in an aeration tank?
    answer: Bacteria need enough oxygen to break down the load, but blowing more air than needed wastes power, and aeration is often the largest energy user at a treatment plant. Controlling blowers from a DO reading keeps both in balance.
  - question: Optical or membrane sensor?
    answer: Optical sensors need less routine work, as there is no membrane or electrolyte to replace and no flow is needed past the sensor. Membrane sensors are well proven and lower in cost, but need regular servicing.
  - question: Where should the sensor go in the tank?
    answer: Somewhere representative of the mixed liquor, away from the diffuser plumes and dead corners, at a depth where it stays submerged. In long tanks, several sensors along the length give better control.

A dissolved oxygen (DO) analyzer measures how much oxygen is dissolved in water. In biological treatment, bacteria use that oxygen to digest the organic load, so DO is the value plant operators watch most closely in an aeration tank. Too little and treatment slows; too much and the blowers waste energy.

The analyzer has two parts: a sensor in the water and a transmitter that displays the reading and sends it on. The sensor works in one of two ways. Optical sensors carry a dye layer that glows when lit; oxygen shortens and dims the glow, and the sensor measures that change. Membrane sensors let oxygen pass through a thin membrane into an electrolyte, where it creates a current in proportion to its amount.

Oxygen solubility depends on temperature and salinity, and the reading also relates to air pressure, so the analyzer compensates for these.

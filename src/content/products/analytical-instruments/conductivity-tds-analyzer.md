A conductivity and TDS analyzer tells you how much dissolved salt is in water. Pure water conducts electricity poorly; dissolved minerals and salts break into charged ions that carry current. Measuring conductivity therefore gives a fast, continuous indication of purity or concentration.

TDS, or total dissolved solids, is the weight of material dissolved in the water. The analyzer estimates it from conductivity using a conversion factor, which is why it suits RO plants, water ATMs and boiler and cooling water control.

Two sensor types are common. Contacting sensors pass a small alternating current between two or four electrodes and suit clean water, down to very low levels. Inductive, or toroidal, sensors induce a current through the liquid without exposed electrodes and suit dirty, corrosive or very salty liquids. Either way, the reading is temperature-compensated, and the analyzer shows it, signals it and can switch relays.

faqs:
  - question: What is the difference between a single-jet and a multi-jet meter?
    answer: A single-jet meter drives its vane wheel with one jet of water. A multi-jet meter splits the flow into several jets around the wheel, which balances the load on it and usually helps it last and hold its accuracy at low flows.
  - question: What is a Woltmann meter?
    answer: A mechanical meter with a helical turbine lying along the pipe, rather than a vane wheel. It passes large flows with little pressure loss, which is why it is used on bigger, usually flanged, supply lines.
  - question: Does a dry dial meter read better than a wet dial?
    answer: It mostly stays readable for longer. In a dry dial meter the register is sealed away from the water, so its glass does not cloud or scale over. Wet dial meters are simpler and suit clean water.

    answer: Many accept a pulse output or a reed switch on the register, which can feed a counter, data logger or building management system.

A mechanical water meter measures water using nothing but the force of the water itself. Inside the body, a moving element turns at a speed set by the flow, and a train of gears carries that motion to a register that counts up the total, much like a car's odometer.

The moving element varies with the type. Jet meters, single or multi-jet, aim the water at a small vane wheel and suit service connections and smaller lines. Woltmann meters place a helical turbine along the pipe axis, giving the high capacity bulk supply lines need. In most designs a magnetic coupling transmits the motion through a sealed wall to a dry register.

With no power supply, battery or software, a mechanical meter is easy to install almost anywhere and simple to read. Its accuracy at very low flows is lower than an electronic meter's, and the moving parts slowly wear.

faqs:
  - question: How does the pointer move if the tube is solid metal?
    answer: The float carries a magnet. A follower magnet in the indicator housing outside the tube tracks it and turns the pointer. Nothing passes through the tube wall, so there is no seal to leak.
  - question: Can it give an electrical signal?
    answer: Yes. The indicator can be fitted with a transmitter for a 4–20 mA output, often with HART, and with limit switches for high and low flow alarms. The mechanical pointer still works if the power is off.
  - question: Does it always have to be mounted vertically?
    answer: The standard design is vertical with flow upwards. Spring-loaded versions are made for horizontal lines or downward flow, so say how the pipe runs when you enquire.
  - question: When is a metal tube rotameter better than an electronic flow meter?
    answer: When you want a reliable local reading with no power, low cost per point and good tolerance of temperature and pressure, for example on purge, seal and utility lines. For higher accuracy or totalising, a vortex, electromagnetic or mass flow meter is the better tool.

A metal tube rotameter works on the same variable area principle as a glass one, but everything in contact with the fluid is metal. A float moves inside a tapered metal tube, or a cone moves through an orifice, and rises as the flow increases. Since the float cannot be seen, a magnet in it drives a pointer on a dial outside the tube.

The all-metal build is what earns it a place. It carries steam, hot oil, high-pressure gas and aggressive chemicals safely, and it does not matter whether the fluid is clear or black. There is no glass to break, which also makes it the accepted choice where a leak would be dangerous.

The basic meter is purely mechanical, which suits remote points and hazardous areas. Add a transmitter and the same instrument feeds a control system as well. For clear liquids and gases at low pressure, where a direct view of the float is enough, the glass or acrylic [rotameter](/products/flow-meters/rotameter/) costs less.

faqs:
  - question: What information is needed to bore an orifice plate?
    answer: The fluid, the maximum, normal and minimum flow, the working pressure and temperature, the density or molecular weight, the pipe size and schedule, and the differential pressure range you want at full flow. The bore is calculated from these, so the plate is made for one duty.
  - question: What else is needed to get a flow reading?
    answer: A differential pressure transmitter connected to the two tappings through impulse lines and a valve manifold. The transmitter or the control system takes the square root of the differential pressure to give flow. On steam and gas, pressure and temperature compensation improves the result.
  - question: How much straight pipe does it need?
    answer: More than most meters. A good run of straight pipe upstream and a shorter one downstream are needed for the flow profile to settle, and the length depends on the fittings ahead and the bore ratio. Tell us the layout and we will advise.
  - question: What are the drawbacks of an orifice plate?
    answer: It causes a permanent pressure loss, its useful flow range is narrow at about 3 or 4 to 1, and the sharp edge wears with dirty or wet fluids, so it should be inspected from time to time.

An orifice plate is a thin metal disc with a hole bored in it, clamped across the pipe between two flanges. Fluid has to speed up to get through the hole, and as it does, its pressure falls. The difference between the pressure just upstream and just downstream rises with the square of the flow, so measuring that difference gives the flow rate.

The assembly consists of the plate, a pair of orifice flanges with pressure tappings drilled in them, gaskets and bolting. A tab on the plate stands clear of the flanges and carries the bore size and tag details, so the plate can be identified without opening the line. A [differential pressure transmitter](/products/pressure-instruments/differential-pressure-transmitter/) piped to the tappings completes the measurement.

There are no moving parts, the method works on steam, gases and liquids at almost any pressure and temperature, and the cost changes little with pipe size. Those strengths keep it in wide use, especially on steam and on large lines. Where a wider flow range is wanted, compare it with a [vortex flow meter](/products/flow-meters/vortex-flow-meter/).

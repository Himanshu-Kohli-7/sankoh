faqs:
  - question: Do I need a weir or flume?
    answer: Yes, for an accurate reading. The flume or weir gives a known relation between depth and flow. Without one, the meter can only estimate flow from the channel shape and slope, which is far less certain.
  - question: Weir or Parshall flume?
    answer: A weir is cheap and accurate but causes a drop in level and traps silt behind it. A Parshall flume loses little head and lets solids pass, so it is the usual choice for sewage and effluent. Tell us the channel width, flow range and what the water carries.
  - question: How is this different from an ultrasonic flow meter on a pipe?
    answer: A clamp-on or inline ultrasonic meter measures velocity in a pipe running full. An open channel meter measures the level of a free surface and needs the channel, or a partly full pipe, to be open to the air above.
  - question: Can the readings be logged or sent to a remote system?
    answer: Most units totalise and show flow locally and give a 4–20 mA or RS-485 output. Data loggers and GSM or 4G modems are available for remote monitoring and reporting.

An open channel flow meter measures water that flows with a free surface: in a channel, a drain or a pipe that is not running full. It does this by measuring depth. A non-contact sensor mounted above the water sends ultrasonic pulses down to the surface and times the echoes to find the level. The transmitter then works out the flow rate from that level and keeps a running total.

The conversion relies on a primary element, usually a Parshall flume or a weir built into the channel. Each shape has a known relation between the head of water and the flow, so a given level always means the same flow.

Because nothing touches the water, there are no parts to clog with solids or fibres. Mount the sensor at the point the flume's design calls for, out of direct sun and spray. For level alone, see the [ultrasonic level transmitter](/products/level-instruments/ultrasonic-level-transmitter/). For pipes that run full, an [electromagnetic flow meter](/products/flow-meters/electromagnetic-flow-meter/) is the usual choice.

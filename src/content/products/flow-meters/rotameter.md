faqs:
  - question: Why must a rotameter be installed vertically?
    answer: The float is held up by the flow against its own weight, so the tube has to stand upright with flow entering at the bottom. Mounted off vertical, the float drags on the tube and the reading is wrong.
  - question: Can a rotameter calibrated for water be used on another liquid?
    answer: Not directly. The scale is made for one fluid at a stated density, viscosity, temperature and, for gases, pressure. A different fluid or different conditions need a correction factor or a new scale, so give the actual fluid and conditions when you order.
  - question: When should I pick a glass tube and when an acrylic body?
    answer: Acrylic body rotameters are economical and tough, and suit water and air at modest pressure and temperature, as on RO skids. Glass tube rotameters handle a wider range of chemicals and temperatures and read more finely. For opaque, hot or high-pressure fluids, use a metal tube rotameter.
  - question: How accurate is a rotameter?
    answer: It is an indicator more than a precision meter, typically within a few percent of full scale. That is enough for setting and checking a flow. For billing or tight control, use an electromagnetic, turbine or mass flow meter.

A rotameter is the simplest way to see how much is flowing. Inside it is a tapered tube, narrow at the bottom and wider at the top, with a float free to move up and down. Flow entering from below lifts the float. As the float rises, the gap around it grows, until the upward force of the flow just balances the float's weight. The float settles at that height, and the flow rate is read off the scale beside it.

Because the reading comes from the float's position, the meter needs no power, wiring or signal converter, and the pressure drop across it stays small and nearly constant. The fluid has to be clear enough to see the float through, and the meter must stand vertically.

Two constructions cover most needs. Acrylic body rotameters are moulded or machined from a clear block and are common on water treatment skids. Glass tube rotameters have a separate glass tube held between end fittings, often with a protective cover. For steam, opaque liquids and higher pressures, see the [metal tube rotameter](/products/flow-meters/metal-tube-rotameter/).

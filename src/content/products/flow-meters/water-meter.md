A water meter records how much water has passed through a line, usually as a running total in cubic metres or litres. Fitted on an incoming supply, it shows what a site is drawing. Fitted on branches, it shows which building, department or machine is using it, which is often the first step in cutting waste.

Most water meters fall into two groups. Mechanical meters turn the flow into motion, through a vane wheel or a turbine, and drive a dial. They are simple, need no power and suit most supply and sub-metering duties. Electronic meters, using the [electromagnetic](/products/flow-meters/electromagnetic-flow-meter/) or [ultrasonic](/products/flow-meters/ultrasonic-flow-meter/) principle, have nothing moving in the bore and keep their accuracy at very low flows.

Install the meter where it stays full of water, with isolating valves either side so it can be removed easily.

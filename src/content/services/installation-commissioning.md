Installing a valve is more than bolting it into a line. It has to face the right way for the flow, sit square between its flanges without strain, and have room around it for the handwheel or actuator to be reached and maintained. Instruments need the right orientation and a straight run of pipe where the measuring principle calls for one.

Automated valves add a second layer. The actuator needs a clean, dry air supply or the correct power, the limit switches and positioner must be set to the real travel, and the control system must see the valve move when it sends a signal. Commissioning is where these are checked together, one valve at a time, before the plant depends on them.

We take care of both layers and keep you informed as the work goes along. If something on the existing line turns out to need repair, our [valve repair](/valve-repair/) service can deal with it on site.
